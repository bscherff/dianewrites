About the Author

Diane Hagan Scherff

Diane Hagan Scherff grew up walking the oak-lined squares of Savannah, Georgia — a city that seemed to hold time gently, like pressed flowers between the pages of a book. Her debut work, Girls to Pearls, draws from those years and the memories of young women who came of age in an era of tradition, faith, and quiet becoming.

A storyteller at heart, Diane blends factual history with personal memory to illuminate the lives of the girls who passed through the halls of St. Vincent's Academy in the 1950s. Girls to Pearls is a tribute to their resilience, their laughter, and the instructors who guided them — a portrait of a place and a generation that shaped the women they would grow to be.

The Book

Girls to Pearls

Historical Narrative Non-Fiction

In the heart of 1950s Savannah, Georgia, a group of Catholic girls navigates the rituals of girlhood inside the walls of St. Vincent's Academy. Under the watchful care of their religious instructors, they learn more than lessons — they learn who they are, and who they might become. Girls to Pearls is their story: warm, honest, and deeply Southern.

Part memoir, part history, Girls to Pearls weaves together personal recollection and period research to offer a vivid portrait of a city and an era. It is a book about tradition and change, about the small rebellions and quiet devotions that shape a life — told with the affection of someone who was there.

Setting: Savannah, Georgia — 1950s School: St. Vincent's Academy
